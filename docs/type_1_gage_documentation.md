## What is a Type-1 Gage test

A type 1 gage test is a test designed to look at the repeatability of a measurement system. This type of testing requires a measurement standard, ideally more than 25 measurements, and is carried out by one operator. 

A Type-1 gage study evaluates the measurement system at one point in the operating range under ideal conditions.

The type 1 gage test tells you:

- The capabilities of your measurement system: Repeatability and bias relative to a measurement standard.
- If your measurement system has signficant bias (Deviation from measurement standard)
- If your measurement system has acceptable variation based on your specified tolerance

## Example of a Type-1 Gage test

Logan has a conductivity probe that needs to be accurate +/- 0.1 mS/cm of its measurement. The total tolerance for this conductivity probe is therefore 0.2 mS/cm (Upper limit - Lower limit). He has a measurement standard solution provided by the manufacturer that has a conductivity of 12.88 mS/cm. 

Logan measured the conductivity of his standard 25 different times on his conductivity probe. He found that there was signficant bias in his conductivity probe, as it was on average reading 0.25 mS/cm above the measurement standard. 

Additionally it had unnacceptable variation, with the spread of the data spanning +/- 0.5 mS/cm. This conductivity probe was deemed incapable from the type-1 gage test, and Logan decided to order a new one.

## When to do a Type-1 Gage test?

As stated above you need to have the following in order to carry out a Type-1 gage test:

- Reliable calibration standard, or validated standard for your measurement system
- Known tolerances for your instrument (Either from the manufacturer or determined in-house)
- One operator

Type-1 gage testing is fantastic for:

- Troubleshooting an instrument
- Validating a new instrument
- Sanity check following calibration/maintenance 

## What a Type-1 Gage test does NOT tell you

Type-1 gage testing does not tell you:

- Reproducibility - Operator to operator variation
- Measurement drift over time - Traditionally carried out in quick succession
- Linearity - Only checked at a single standard (Ex: For a 12.88 mS/cm standard, it won't tell you if the conductivity probe is accurate at 1.418 mS/cm for a non-linear calibration curve)
