## User Stores for Gage R&R Companion

Terry is a quality engineer. They want to quantify the reliability of a measurement system/tool across different measurements and users. They want to know hwo much variance is coming from the tool and how much can be attributed to different users. They are college educated with a technical degree.

John is a technician who is working on a Gage R&R project assigned to him. They want to take their data from the project and analyze it using the Gage R&R app. They need it to be able to give out the reproducibility and repeatability to report back to their manager without needing to ask for the project plan first. They are relatively technical and are able to do pointy clicky.

Albert is a team manager. He wants to use the tool to see how viable a new measurement tool is. He wants to focus on how different people on his team perform with the tool. He is an upper manager with an MBA but no real technical training.

Paul is an R&D engineer for a small company where he is the sole user of an instrument. He wants to use the tool to figure out what type of test run to quantify his instruments repeatability. He wants to be certain that he's using the tool right, and wants a simple interface. He is very technically skilled with his instrument, but doesn't know much about gage testing.

Abby is a process engineer. There is a new tool being implemented and he wants to see how much reliable the new tool is compared to the old method. He wants a quick and easy method to tell him. He is an engineer with a lot of hands on experience.

Sara is the CEO of a successful tech startup that produces membranes for gas separations. Recently, her membranes have been reported to have variable thickness. Since she just brought on 5 new production technicians, she wants to assess the process variation and parse out if its a result of poor reproducibility between operators, or poor repeatibility of the thickness measurement system. She wants to get to the bottom of this as fast as possible, and wants it to be easy. She has an advanced degree in engineering.

Jeanne is a process engineer. She wants to know if the change in her experiment is due to actual changes in the experiment or due to the measurement error. She wants to be able to determine this quickly to move further into the experiment, and to be able to tell if the error due to measuring is small enough. She is very technical, however has no clue on gage testing.

Bobart is a manager. He does not want to get deep into what goes into Gage R&R testing, he just wants to know how to fix it. He wants to be able to access a Gage R&R test somehow and have it tell him root causes of why error is so high (or not high). He is very non-technical and does not know about gage testing.

Robert is a technician. He was assigned by his boss to identify the variability of his viscosity measurement. He wants to know how to test his viscometer to see if he can trust the readout he gets from his instrument. He wants to know exactly what to do for his test, and wants it to be easy. He is fresh out of undergrad with a mechanical engineering degree, but doesn't have any work experience.

Stephanie is a quality engineer at a large company. She knows that she has to carry out a nested Gage R&R to quantify the measurement error of her system. She loves to use Minitab, but her company deemed it wasn't worth the price. She is fluent in Minitab functionality, and really doesn't want to have to learn another new software. She has 10 years work experienve as a QC engineer for an aerospace.

Steve is a manufacturing engineer. He uses a quality control test whether his product is up to spec or not. He wants to identify the variability in his QC test to assess if he can trust its result. He wants a test to assess the different sources of error, and wants to know all the details. He is very technically savvy, and is familiar with Gage R&R. 

May is the head of an engineering team. She is incharge of selecting a new tool to use to measure something. She has multiple options and wants the one that will be most accurate overall. She wants a quick, easy, and simple interface that tells her the overall varibaility. She has a technical background and used to be an engineer
Sarah is a quality engineer at a manufacturing plant. Sarah wants to run Gage R&R studies to evaluate measurement system accuracy and repeatability. Sarah wants the tool to automatically calculate results, generate charts, and export reports for audits. Sarah has strong technical and statistical skills but wants a fast, intuitive interface to save time.

Mike is a production technician on the factory floor. Mike wants to input measurement data and quickly see whether a gage passes or fails a Gage R&R study. Mike wants the tool to guide him step-by-step and prevent data entry errors. Mike has moderate technical skills and prefers a simple, visual interface with minimal statistical jargon.

David is an engineering manager responsible for quality compliance. David wants to review Gage R&R results to make decisions about equipment and training. David wants concise summaries, pass/fail indicators, and professional reports suitable for management and customers. David has limited time and moderate technical knowledge, so he values clarity and high-level insights.

Tom is an engineering manager who has worked with Gage R&R in the past. Tom is quitting his job and moving to Tahiti, as part of his offboarding he has to train his replacement on Gage R&R. Tom is very experienced in Gage testing, but his replacement is not. He values a software that is simple to demonstrate with minimal clicks and fast analysis.

Dimitri is a manager working abroad in Mexico working for a company that manufactures laptops. His coworker Josh carried out Gage R&R in their factory, and has kindly asked Dimitri to develop a report based on the findings. Dimitri has an MBA, and has a bachelors in italian history and is not very experienced with Gage R&R. He values a sheet of results that are easily digestible, with a lot of the statistic-backed decision making done for him.

Francesca is a new engineer getting trained by her coworker Tom who is moving to Tahiti. She is to learn how to carry out and design Gage R&R, and part of her onboarding is learning how to analyze Gage R&R data. Tom is not very good at explaining things, so its vital that the software is well documented and easy to learn independently. 

Scooby's 1-week free trial of Minitab expired and he is running a Gage R&R on a piece of equipment that had previously failed testing 6 months prior. He wants to be able to copy and paste values from the output so he can compare the results to the prior R&R testing and provide a meaningful metric to forward to leadership. He knows the ins and outs of this kind of testing, and he wants a user interface that is as clean and easy as Minitab.

Bane is planning out his teams quarterly priorities and wants to ensure that the Gage testing for this quarter is reasonable given other time constraints. He knows key metrics such as the time per test, and costs associated with each test (Based on salaries and consumables). He trusts that the Gage testing his direct-report is planning is going to produce statistically sound results, and really only cares about the money and time associated with it. He values an interface that states how many tests are going to be run per operator, as well as the valuable statistics.

Wesker works at a biotech company and has been tasked with identifying the discrepancy between two batches. He mostly does field work and doesn't know the first thing about troubleshooting. His coworker recommended that he identify all the independent variables associated with the batch, which he concluded were the autosampler and the 5 different operators. His supervisor told him that he should carry out Gage testing. Wesker wants to learn what Gage testing is, what the R&R stand for, and why its helpful for his task. He values thorough documentation that describes the testing, and walks through how to plan one of these experiments.
